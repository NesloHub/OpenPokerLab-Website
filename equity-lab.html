OpenPokerLab • PokerEquity Lab v1.0.0 • Free open-source equity calculator

PokerStove-class equity, rebuilt for today

PokerEquity Lab is a free Texas Hold'em equity and range calculator for Windows. Put two to ten players on a board, paint their ranges in a 13x13 matrix, and get win, tie, lose and equity numbers that are exact when the scenario is small and sampled up to five million runouts when it is not. It replaces the discontinued PokerStove with a current build for Windows 10 and 11 — no installer required, no runtime to download, no account, and no network calls at all.

What is inside

Everything an equity calculator needs

Range versus range, preflop or on a flop, turn and river — with a range editor you can draw with the mouse instead of typing.

⚖️

Precise equity for 2 to 10 players

Every combo and every runout is enumerated exactly when the scenario is small. When it is too big to enumerate, the engine switches to a multi threaded Monte Carlo sample — and tells you which one it used.

🎨

A 13x13 matrix you paint

Drag across the grid to add hands, drag again to take them away. The matrix, the combo counter ("290 combos · 21.9%") and the range text stay in sync in both directions.

🖌

Paint weights of 100 / 75 / 50 / 25%

Pick a paint weight and drag, or type the frequency: JJ+:50, AKs:25. Partly weighted cells show their weight in the corner, so you can mix a range the way you actually play it.

⚡

Quick range presets

Top 5%, 12%, 20% and 30%, pairs, suited aces, broadways, suited connectors and gappers, any two suited — and Random hand, which fills all 1326 combos at 100% just like PokerStove.

🃏

Board builder & hand readout

Fill the flop, turn and river from the palette; cards already in play are dimmed so a card can never be dealt twice. Every player gets a plain English readout of the current hand, for example Two pair, aces and kings.

📊

Numbers you can reproduce

Win, tie, lose and equity per player, coloured red below 30%, amber around 50% and green above 70%. Choose 10,000 to 5,000,000 runouts, set a seed for a repeatable sample, and read the elapsed time in the status bar.

Your first equity number in two minutes

PokerEquity Lab is a self-contained Windows build. There is nothing to install, no .NET runtime to download and no account to create — and nothing is written anywhere but in the folder you unzip it into.

  • 1
    Unpack the portable package
    Right-click the ZIP and choose Extract All. Anywhere will do — Desktop, a tools folder or a USB stick. You get PokerEquityLab.App.exe and a short README.txt. Prefer a normal installation? Run the setup instead: it installs into your user profile with a Start Menu shortcut and an uninstaller, and it needs no admin rights.
  • 2
    Build the scenario
    Click the first card slot of Player 1 and pick two cards from the palette, or leave the slots empty and just describe the range. Do the same for Player 2. Use + Add and Remove to play between 2 and 10 opponents.
  • 3
    Press Calculate
    Every player gets an equity percentage, win / tie / lose, and a plain English hand readout. The highest equity is highlighted, and the status bar tells you the mode, the number of runouts and how long it took.

💡 Pro-tip: start from a preset

Click Top 30% for one player and Random hand for the other, then press Calculate. Five seconds of work and you already have a baseline read — refine the range from there by painting the matrix.

JJ+, AKs, AQs+, KQs:50

Draw the range instead of typing it

The 13x13 grid is the range editor. The diagonal holds the pairs, the cells above it are suited hands (AKs) and the cells below it are offsuit hands (AKo). The counter in the top right corner tells you exactly how big the range is: 290 combos · 21.9%.

  • ✓
    Painting: hold the left mouse button and drag across the grid to add hands, drag across painted cells to take them away. The range text below the matrix updates the moment you release the button.
  • ✓
    Weights: choose Paint weight 100%, 75%, 50% or 25% and then drag — a cell painted below 100% keeps its weight in the corner. Painted and typed weights mix freely, and :50 is the same as :0.5.
  • ✓
    Presets: the buttons beside the matrix drop in a ready-made range — Top 5 / 12 / 20 / 30%, pairs (small, medium, big), suited aces, broadways, suited connectors, suited gappers and any two suited.
  • ✓
    Random, Clear range, Clear: Random fills the selected player with every hand at 100% (the PokerStove and Equilab default), Clear range empties one player, and Clear resets every range and the board.

🔁 The matrix and the text are the same range

Paint a range and the text box writes itself; paste a range into the text box and the matrix repaints. Nothing is ever hidden in one of the two views.

The range language in one page

A range is a comma separated list of hands. Pairs are written as two equal ranks, suited hands get an s and offsuit hands an o; leave the suffix out to mean both. A weight is appended with a colon, and a single card on its own means "this card plus any other card".

Example What it means Size
AA, AKs, AKo A pair, a suited hand, an offsuit hand 6 / 4 / 12 combos
AK Suited and offsuit: both are included 16 combos
JJ+ JJ, QQ, KK, AA — every pair from JJ upwards 24 combos
AQs+ AQs, AKs — same high card, better kickers 8 combos
22-99 Every pair from 22 through 99 48 combos
T9s-54s Suited connectors with the same one card gap, T9s down to 54s 24 combos
A5s-A2s Suited aces with a kicker from 5 down to 2 16 combos
AsKh One exact combination: the ace of spades and the king of hearts 1 combo
As Every combination that contains the ace of spades 51 combos
JJ+:50 Half weight for every combo of JJ+ (also written JJ+:0.5) 24 combos at 50%
random or * Every hand at 100% — the "any two cards" range 1326 combos

Combos and percentages

There are 1326 possible starting hands and the matrix counter always shows how many of them your range contains — both as a combo count and as a percentage of all hands. A range of "290 combos" is the 21.9% you see in the corner of the matrix, which makes it easy to compare your range with the charts you study.

Weights scale the combos: painting a hand at 50% counts it as half a combo, exactly like a solver frequency. That is how you build a mixed range such as JJ+:50, AKo:25 without leaving the calculator.

📋 Copy a range into the box

A range copied from your notes, your charts or a solver export usually works as-is — paste it into the range box and the matrix follows. If something cannot be parsed, the app says so under the box instead of guessing.

Put a board down and see who is ahead

The board row at the top is a flop, a turn and a river waiting to happen. Press a slot, then click a card in the palette — the board stays contiguous, so you can never leave a hole in the middle of a flop.

  • 🃏
    The board: F1, F2 and F3 are the flop, then Trn and Rvr. The label next to the row tells you where you are (Preflop, Flop, Turn, River) and the mode hint explains what the engine will do. Clear board empties it again.
  • 🂡
    The palette: 52 cards in a four colour deck — black spades, red hearts, blue diamonds and green clubs, with the rank large in the middle and the suit in the corner. Cards that are already in play (a hole card or the board) are dimmed, so a card can never appear twice. Empty slot clears the selected slot.
  • 📈
    The result: after Calculate every player row shows the equity percentage, win / tie / lose, and a readout of the current hand such as Two pair, aces and kings. Equity is coloured red below 30%, amber around 50% and green above 70%, and the player in front is highlighted.
  • 📊
    The status bar: the bottom line reports what happened, for example Equity calculated. Player 1 69.23% · Player 2 30.77% · Monte Carlo · 200,000 runouts · 0.08 s. If a range is empty or a card is missing the bar says so instead of showing a number.

🃏 Reading a flop spot

Give both players a range, put a flop down, and press Calculate: you get the range versus range equity as well as the current hand each side actually holds. Add the turn or the river and the numbers tighten — the same scenario, one street further.

Modes, numbers and what is inside the ZIP

Two settings decide how the engine works: the mode and the number of runouts. Everything else is a convenience you can ignore until you need it.

  • ✓
    Mode Auto: exact enumeration when the scenario is small, random sampling when it is not. This is the default and the right choice for almost everything.
  • ✓
    Mode Exact: every combo and every runout is enumerated — no approximation. AA against KK comes out as 81.9% because all of it was counted.
  • ✓
    Mode Monte Carlo: always samples, using the runout count you pick: 10,000, 50,000, 200,000 (the default), 1,000,000 or 5,000,000. Sampling error falls with the square root of the count, so 5M runouts is about five times tighter than 200,000.
  • ✓
    Seed: leave the box empty and every calculation is a fresh sample; type an integer such as 12345 and you get the same sample back, which is useful when you compare two ranges.
  • ✓
    Inside the portable ZIP: PokerEquityLab.App.exe and a short README.txt — no DLLs to keep together. Unzip it anywhere and delete the folder to remove the app again. The installer instead puts the app in your user profile with a Start Menu entry and an uninstaller; neither version needs admin rights.
  • ✓
    Verify your download: SHA256SUMS.txt lists the SHA-256 hash of the ZIP and the installer. Run certutil -hashfile "PokerEquityLab-1.0.0-win-x64.zip" SHA256 in a command prompt and compare. The build is not code signed, so Windows SmartScreen may ask for confirmation the first time you start it: choose More info and then Run anyway.

🧮 One engine, two layers

The cards, ranges, hand evaluator and equity engine live in a library without any user interface, and the window is a thin layer on top of it. That is why the numbers can be tested against known values, and why the same code builds for other desktops: the published download is the Windows x64 build, and Linux or macOS builds can be produced from source with the .NET 10 SDK.

Fair play

Where an equity calculator fits in the rules

An equity calculator is a study tool. PokerEquity Lab is a normal offline desktop program: it has no connection to a poker client, no overlay, no automation and no server. Poker rooms still differ on what you may run while you play, so the honest answer is below.

Criterion Live assistance tools PokerEquity Lab (OpenPokerLab)
Connection to a poker client ❌ Trackers and HUDs attach to the client ✓ Nothing — the app never looks for a poker room
Process access, hooks or injection ❌ Reads client memory ✓ Zero — no access to other processes
Network activity ⚠️ Licence checks, hand uploads, cloud sync ✓ 100% offline — no account, no telemetry
Real-time assistance (RTA) ❌ Live solver hints and strategy advice ✓ It computes a number when you press Calculate
Automation ❌ Can click, bet or fold for you ✓ There is nothing that could play a hand
Studying away from the table ⚠️ Often restricted ✓ The intended use — review hands, learn equities
Running it during real money play ❌ Banned almost everywhere ⚠️ Depends on the room — check the current rules first

Tools like PokerStove, Equilab and PokerEquity Lab are widely treated as study software, but "widely" is not a licence. Read the third-party tools section of your room's terms before you use any calculator during play, and never use one as a live decision aid.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PokerEquity Lab meant to replace PokerStove? ▼
For day-to-day equity work, yes. It does the same job — range versus range equity, preflop and postflop — in a build that runs on current Windows. You get more players (up to 10), a range matrix you can paint with the mouse, paint weights of 100 / 75 / 50 / 25% for mixed frequencies, 15 one-click presets, a board builder and a window that scales down to small screens. PokerStove's last Windows snapshot is from another era; this is the same idea, maintained.
Do I have to install .NET or any other runtime? ▼
No. The portable ZIP and the installer both contain everything the app needs, including the .NET runtime it was built with: unzip, run PokerEquityLab.App.exe, done. Nothing is installed into the system, no admin rights are needed and there is no library to register.
How accurate are the numbers? ▼
Exact when the scenario is small enough to enumerate, and there the answer is not an estimate: AA against KK is 81.9%, counted runout by runout. Otherwise the engine samples and labels the result as Monte Carlo, with the error shrinking like one over the square root of the runout count — 200,000 runouts land within a few tenths of a percent, and you can raise it to 5,000,000. Set a seed if you want the exact same sample again.
Can I check that the download is intact? ▼
Yes. SHA256SUMS.txt next to the downloads lists the SHA-256 hash of both download files (and of version.txt). In a command prompt, run certutil -hashfile "PokerEquityLab-1.0.0-win-x64.zip" SHA256 and compare the result with the file. The build is not code signed yet, so SmartScreen can show a warning on first start — that is the signature, not the checksum, missing.
Is there a version that needs no download at all? ▼
Yes — the browser-based Poker Equity Calculator and the Interactive Range Maker on this site cover quick pot odds, equities and range building without installing anything. PokerEquity Lab is the desktop version: up to 10 players, exact enumeration, painted weights, presets and a full board, all offline and much faster on big scenarios.
Does it work on macOS or Linux, and is the source available? ▼
The published download is the Windows x64 build (Windows 10 version 1809 or newer, and Windows 11). The engine is a plain library without user interface dependencies and the window is built with Avalonia, so the project also builds for Linux and macOS from source with the .NET 10 SDK — OpenPokerLab ships the Windows build first.
Can I use it while I am playing? ▼
That depends on your poker room, not on the program. PokerEquity Lab is an offline calculator: it is not attached to a client, it reads nothing and it never acts for you, which is why tools in this family are usually allowed for study. Many rooms still forbid running any calculator during real money play, so check their third-party tools rules — the fair play table above spells out the difference between studying and live assistance.
